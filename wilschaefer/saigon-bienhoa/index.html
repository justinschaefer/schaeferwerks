Sai Gon + Bien Hoa

SP4 WIL SCHAEFER · 45TH MED DET · DATE UNCONFIRMED

This category is genuinely two slide trays merged into one: a title card at #1 and a second, separate title card at #88, both reading "Sai-Gon + Bien Hoa." The first reel is a walking tour of central Saigon — markets, streets, a likely match for Bến Thành Market's clock tower — drifting into Bien Hoa by its end. The second reel picks up specifically in Bien Hoa: construction, a French cemetery, a gas station. Slides 5–12, 46, 48–52, and 55–87 were never scanned, lost, or belong to a different reel entirely.

#17
"Lincoln Library"

The building's sign, legible in the full-size scan, reads "Thư Viện Abraham Lincoln" — the Abraham Lincoln Library, USIS's cultural outreach institution in Saigon. This building is the Rex Complex, which housed the library from 1962 until it relocated in 1964 — a window that overlaps his own tour. Still standing today, same corner, as the Rex Hotel.

#18
"Building Admin"

The sign reads "Tòa Đô Chánh" — Saigon City Hall, officially named that during 1954–1975, exactly his tour period. Built 1898–1909 by the French as the Hôtel de Ville de Saigon; renamed the Ho Chi Minh City People's Committee Building after 1975. Still standing, same site, about 250 meters from the Abraham Lincoln Library in slide #17 — both shot on the same walk through downtown. The same building appears again, from a side street, in slide #14.

#19
"Trug Hung Dau - Ly Loi Market"

A market building with a distinctive clock tower. The streets he names — Trần Hưng Đạo and Lê Lợi — still border Bến Thành Market today, and the arched gate and tower shape are an exact match to that market's south entrance, essentially unchanged since. Confirmed.

#22
"Bar-Night Club Restaurant"

A real, named building: the Nguyễn Văn Hảo Building, Saigon's Art Deco "Flatiron," built in the 1920s–30s for automotive-parts businessman Nguyễn Văn Hảo, at the triangular junction of Trần Hưng Đạo, Ký Con, and Yersin streets in District 1, near Bến Thành Market — original address 19-21 Boulevard Galliéni. The same Nguyễn Văn Hảo also funded the nearby Nguyễn Văn Hảo Theatre, which in 1945 hosted the public meeting that launched the August Revolution in the south. Descendants of the family reportedly still live in the building today.

#31
"Bridge to Can Oi, 3 Cops"

A monumental bridge on sculpted concrete piers, three police officers visible near the near bank. "Can Oi" is almost certainly his phonetic spelling of Khánh Hội, a real district across the Bến Nghé Creek — its old bridge was demolished in 1961 and replaced by the reinforced-concrete Khánh Hội Bridge, built just a couple of years before this photo. That specific bridge no longer exists (demolished 2009), so this isn't a pixel-confirmed match, but the timing and construction style line up well.
